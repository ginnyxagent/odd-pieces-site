Important distinction

UGC vs CGC, plain English

These terms sound more confusing than they need to be. Here is the simple difference.

UGC

Creator ad content

Someone is hired to make content for the brand. Even if it feels natural, it is still more planned and more structured.

  • Better when you want exact hooks and talking points
  • Better for testing specific angles
  • Feels more like: “let me show you this product”
CGC

Customer-feeling content

This should feel like a normal person discovered the product and wanted to share it. Less polished, less ad-like, more believable.

  • Better when you want trust and relatability
  • Better for gifting, discovery, and skeptical-friend angles
  • Feels more like: “okay wait, I found this weird thing”
Reusable intro block

Product explainer, use this in every brief

If the person making the asset does not understand the product, the output gets generic fast.

Copy and paste

Odd Pieces product explainer

Odd Pieces makes story-first mystery puzzles. These are not normal jigsaw puzzles. The box shows the BEFORE scene. The puzzle you build is the same scene 15 seconds later, after everything has gone weird. Inside the box there is also a sealed Secret Ending Envelope that you only open once the puzzle is complete. So the product is not just “a nice puzzle image.” It is a reveal experience. A surprise. A twist. A table reaction. People buy Odd Pieces because it feels more surprising, more social, and more memorable than an ordinary puzzle. It also works really well as a gift because people have not seen something quite like it before. The brand tone is clever, punchy, playful, slightly unhinged, and direct. Never corporate. Never generic. Never bland.

Language guardrails

What to say and what not to say

This protects the brand from sounding like every bland ad on the internet.

Say this
  • Not your ordinary puzzle
  • The box is the before, the puzzle is 15 seconds later
  • Secret Ending Envelope
  • Twist, reveal, surprise
  • Gift people talk about
  • Wildly fun and unexpected
  • You think you know what’s happening. You don’t.
Do not say this
  • Fun for the whole family
  • Immersive puzzle experience
  • Premium quality guaranteed
  • Hours of entertainment
  • Perfect holiday gift
  • Luxury game night essential
  • Anything that sounds like a generic brand manager wrote it
Template 1

UGC creator brief

Use this for paid creators filming fresh content specifically for ads. This should read like a normal-person creative brief, not marketing homework.

How to frame this for creators

Your job is not to explain every detail

Your job is to make someone stop scrolling and think: wait, what is this? This product is fun to talk about because it is actually weird and different. Lean into that.

UGC Brief Template

What to send the creator

What this product is, in plain English
Odd Pieces is a puzzle brand, but not a normal one. The box shows one scene. The puzzle is that same scene 15 seconds later, after everything has gone off the rails. Then there is a sealed Secret Ending Envelope in the box that you only open when you finish. So the whole product is built around surprise, reveal, and people reacting to the ending.
Why this is fun content to make
You are not trying to sell a boring product. You are showing people something they probably have not seen before. The best videos feel like: “okay wait, this is actually kind of brilliant.”
What we want the viewer to feel
Curious. Surprised. Slightly obsessed. Like they need to know what the twist is.
Audience
[Cold / Warm / Hot]
Angle
[Choose one: twist, before/after, gift, envelope, table reaction, not your ordinary puzzle]
How to think about the video
Talk like you are telling a friend about a weirdly good thing you found online. Natural. Clear. A little amused. Not “hey guys I’m so excited to partner with...” energy.
Hook ideas
1. This is not a normal puzzle. 2. I thought this was a regular puzzle and I was very wrong. 3. The box shows the before. The puzzle is what happens 15 seconds later. 4. There is literally a secret ending hidden inside this box. 5. This is the first puzzle I’ve seen that actually has a plot twist. 6. If you’re bored by normal puzzles, this is the one to look at.
What to film
Start with your face if possible. Then show the box clearly. Then show puzzle details or pieces on the table. Then tease the Secret Ending Envelope. Then end with your reaction or your recommendation.
Tone
Natural, clever, deadpan, curious, slightly obsessed. You can be funny, but don’t perform. The best version feels like you genuinely think the product is cool.
Do say things like
“this is not a normal puzzle” / “the box is the before” / “there’s a secret ending in the box” / “this would be such a good gift” / “I’ve never seen a puzzle do this before”
Do not say things like
“premium puzzle experience” / “fun for the whole family” / “hours of entertainment” / “high-quality materials” unless that is the whole point of your video, which it usually is not
Simple filming tips
Good natural light. Clear audio. Keep the first line strong. Don’t spend 8 seconds introducing yourself. Don’t bury the interesting part. Slightly imperfect is okay if it feels real.
Deliverables
3 hook variations, 15 to 30 seconds each, vertical 9:16, clean audio, no copyrighted music, and give us a few different opening lines if possible.

Template 2

CGC / customer-style creator brief

Use this when the content should feel casual, real, low-pressure, and like a normal person sharing a cool find with another normal person.

How this should feel

Less ad, more “okay, this is actually cool”

CGC should feel discovered, not delivered. The charm is that it feels believable, easy, and human.

CGC Brief Template

What to send the creator

Simple product explanation
Odd Pieces makes puzzles with a twist. The box shows one scene, but the puzzle is that same scene 15 seconds later after everything changes. There is also a Secret Ending Envelope hidden inside the box. So the whole thing feels more like a surprise story than a normal puzzle.
How this content should feel
Like you found something weirdly good and wanted to show a friend. Not polished. Not too scripted. Not trying too hard.
Best CGC energy
Skeptical at first, then impressed. Casual. Honest. Slightly amused. Like, “I did not expect to care about this and now I kind of do.”
Hook ideas
1. Okay wait, this puzzle is actually insane. 2. I genuinely thought this was a normal puzzle. 3. This is the weirdest good gift I’ve found in a while. 4. I’m kind of mad this is such a good idea. 5. Why does this puzzle have a secret ending?
What to film
Handheld selfie intro, casual shot of the box, pieces on a table or couch, natural home setting, maybe your hands flipping through or pointing at details, envelope tease at the end.
What to say
Keep it simple. Explain the before/after mechanic in one or two sentences. Mention the envelope. Mention why it feels different. Mention why it would make a good gift or why it caught your attention.
What to avoid
Big influencer energy. Over-selling. Looking too polished. Sounding like you memorized a script. Acting like you are in a TV ad.
Deliverables
2 to 3 casual versions, 15 to 30 seconds, vertical 9:16, natural audio, real environment, not over-edited.

Creator personas

Pick a creator style before you brief

This makes it much easier for a normal person to know how to perform the ad. Different people sell Odd Pieces in different ways.

Persona 1

The skeptical friend

Energy: “I did not expect to care about this, but honestly this is kind of genius.”

  • Best for cold traffic
  • Works well with twist and before/after angles
  • Feels believable and low-hype
Persona 2

The gift finder

Energy: “This is what you buy when you’re tired of giving boring gifts.”

  • Best for gifting angles
  • Great for Q4, birthdays, hosts, hard-to-shop-for people
  • Feels useful and specific
Persona 3

The puzzle person

Energy: “If you already like puzzles, this is the one that breaks the category open.”

  • Best for audience already interested in puzzles
  • Good for “not your ordinary puzzle” angle
  • More knowledgeable tone is okay
Persona 4

The weirdly good find

Energy: “I found this on the internet and had to show you.”

  • Best for CGC
  • Very shareable tone
  • Feels discoverable, not campaign-y
Persona 5

The date-night / table-night person

Energy: “This is actually such a good thing to do together.”

  • Best for couples, roommates, families, friend groups
  • Good for table reaction angle
  • Focus on shared experience
Persona 6

The delightfully obsessed person

Energy: “The hidden details in this thing are ridiculous.”

  • Best for detail hunters and Easter egg lovers
  • Good for close-up footage
  • Feels nerdy in a fun way

Video editor brief, using existing clips

Editor Brief Template

What the edit needs to do

Footage source
Existing product clips, table footage, founder clips, customer reactions, box shots, puzzle detail shots.
Edit rule
Hook first. No slow warm-up. No long logo intro. No “beautiful montage” before the point lands.
Suggested structure
0 to 2 sec hook 2 to 6 sec product difference 6 to 12 sec visual proof 12 to 20 sec twist / envelope / reaction end with CTA
Caption style
Bold, readable, fast, high contrast, few words per frame. The captions should help the hook land, not narrate every single thing.
What to avoid
Slow pacing, tiny captions, over-designed motion graphics, irrelevant b-roll, long setup, generic stock music energy.
Deliverables
3 to 5 cutdowns, 15 to 30 sec, 9:16 primary, optional 4:5 and 1:1 variants.
Filled example

Editor example brief

Goal: Create 3 cold-audience ads from existing product footage Angle: Before / 15 seconds later Use these clips: - box front shot - puzzle pieces spread on table - close-up puzzle detail shots - envelope shot - founder clip explaining the mechanic Edit flow: - start with text: “The box is the before.” - cut quickly to puzzle build with text: “The puzzle is 15 seconds later.” - show envelope with text: “Then there’s the secret ending.” - end on reaction / founder close with CTA: “See the twist.” Must feel: Clever, surprising, sharp Must not feel: Corporate, sentimental, overproduced
