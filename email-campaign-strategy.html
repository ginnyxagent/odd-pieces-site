Email Campaign Strategy

Odd Pieces should not wait for perfect segmentation before acting. You already know enough about who buys, who actually puzzles, and who turns into a fan. The job now is to run campaigns around customer intent, while quietly collecting better data in the background.

83% of 2025 DTC orders happened in Q4. This is first and foremost a gifting business in season.
2–4% traditional DTC repeat rate. Not because the product fails, but because the buyer is often not the puzzler.
12.1% of Kickstarter backers ever cross over to Shopify. Big warm-audience opportunity sitting there.

The blunt version

  • Gifters need a very different campaign angle than puzzlers.
  • Gift recipients are the highest-upside conversion gap in the business.
  • Loyalists should get insider treatment, not generic promos.
  • Off-season revenue needs leisure positioning, not holiday positioning.
Strategic Spine

What the campaign strategy is actually for

1 · Capture gifting demand

Make Q4 hit harder

Lean into the fact that a huge portion of DTC revenue comes from thoughtful gift-buyers hunting for something clever and memorable.

2 · Convert real puzzlers

Turn recipients into buyers

The end user often is not the purchaser. Campaign strategy should help identify, welcome, and convert the actual puzzler.

3 · Retain the weird little superfans

Grow Odd Club behavior

Repeat customers are disproportionately valuable. Treat them like insiders and they will keep acting like insiders.

4 · Smooth the seasonality

Build non-Q4 demand

Outside gifting windows, the strongest angle is leisure, ritual, mystery, and quality time, not “buy a present.”

Five Campaign Lanes

The core campaign system

Do not send every campaign to everyone with the same framing. Instead, build the program around five repeatable lanes. These are the engine.

Lane 1

Seasonal gifting campaigns

Your biggest revenue lane. Especially Q4, but also Mother’s Day, Father’s Day, birthdays, hostess gifting, White Elephant, and “hard-to-shop-for” moments.

  • Gift guides by recipient type
  • Bundle-focused campaigns
  • Shipping cutoff / urgency sends
  • Why it feels more thoughtful than a generic gift
Lane 2

Product + launch storytelling

These emails are for people who care about the product itself, not just the giftability. Lean into mystery, artwork, reveal, and what makes the puzzle feel special.

  • New release drops
  • Artist / design stories
  • What makes this puzzle weird in the best way
  • If you liked X, try Y next
Lane 3

Gift recipient conversion

The hidden money lane. When someone completes a puzzle or enters the answer-key flow, that is the moment to turn the end user into a customer.

  • Welcome to Odd Pieces
  • Now get one for yourself
  • Next puzzle recommendations
  • Odd Club intro + review + cross-sell
Lane 4

Loyalist retention campaigns

These should feel like insider treatment, not another sale blast. This group buys new releases, uses THEODDCLUB, and actually wants to hear from you.

  • Early access
  • VIP previews
  • Collector / completion emails
  • Vote, share, review, refer
Lane 5

Year-round leisure campaigns

How you reduce overdependence on Q4. Position Odd Pieces as a cozy, screen-free ritual for weekends, rainy days, couples time, and personal unwind time.

  • Weekend plans solved
  • Date night / family night
  • Rainy day / cozy mystery energy
  • Saturday afternoon in the living room

Strategy rule

Q4 campaigns should sell the gift. Launch campaigns should sell the obsession. Recipient campaigns should sell the next purchase. Loyalist campaigns should sell belonging. Off-season campaigns should sell the ritual.

Use the data you already have

How to segment before the fancy segmentation exists

You do not need to wait months for perfect gifter / recipient / puzzler segments. Use behavior as a proxy for intent. Klaviyo already gives you enough to get smarter now.

Cadence

How often to send

Keep it simple. You do not need a bloated campaign machine. You need a consistent one with a clear job.

Baseline

1–2 campaigns per week

  • One revenue-focused send
  • One relationship / brand / story send
  • Enough frequency to stay present without becoming wallpaper
During launches

2–3 campaigns per week

  • Announcement
  • Proof / story / why-it’s-special follow-up
  • Urgency or segmented reminder
During Q4

Increase around tentpoles

  • Gift guide waves
  • Shipping cutoff reminders
  • Recipient-type gift picks
  • Black Friday / Cyber Monday / final gift push
Suggested monthly mix

A workable campaign calendar

This is the simple version, not a giant machine. Enough structure to be strategic, not enough structure to become annoying.

Send 1

Revenue email

  • Gift guide, featured product, bundle push, or seasonal offer
  • Use broad engaged audience + intent-based split where possible
Send 2

Story / proof email

  • Reviews, customer reactions, behind the scenes, artist story, reveal tease
  • Keeps the brand from feeling like pure promo sludge
Optional send 3

Segment-led follow-up

  • Loyalist preview, browse-based reminder, or recipient conversion push
  • Use during launches, holidays, or strong product windows
What matters most

The biggest mistake to avoid

Do not talk to gifters, recipients, puzzlers, and loyalists like they are the same person. They are not. Same list, different motives.

The biggest opportunity to unlock

Use the answer-key and completion moments to identify the real puzzler. That is the bridge from one-time gift business into repeat-customer business.

Recommended operating model

The next few months

Layer 1

Broad campaigns to the engaged list

  • Gift-focused when seasonal
  • Product / mystery / leisure-focused when not seasonal
  • Keep the message clear and singular
Layer 2

Simple behavior-based splits

  • Viewed bundle vs viewed singles
  • Q4-only vs year-round purchasers
